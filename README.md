# Open

*Every post tells you why you're seeing it.*

Open is a prototype social feed that treats algorithmic transparency as a design feature: every post — from an old friend's wedding photo to a paid ad or a public voting reminder — carries an expandable “Why am I seeing this?” explanation showing the signals, data, and economics that put it there. Users can also open “Adjust what you see” to compose their own feed stack, adding single-purpose feeds and filters on top of a strong default baseline, so the system feels legible and controllable rather than opaque and infinite.

**Live app**: https://rebuild2-open-algorithm.lovable.app

Built at Rebuild 2 in Helsinki, August 30th–September 1st, 2026 — 48 hours of prototyping for European
social platforms. Part of [Rebuild 2 Prototypes](https://github.com/rebuild-digital/Rebuild-2-Prototypes).

## The original brief

The unedited voice prompt this prototype was built from, transcribed as spoken during the 48 hours:

> I want to build a prototype that probably should look like a very simple Instagram feed of, uh,
> yeah, uh, somebody's Instagram feed. What I want to explore is, like, I want to put a brand new "Why
> am I seeing this?" button, like, that looks kind of cool and present, and when you click on it, I
> think it should just show you all of the decisions algorithm made in a very clear, really a human
> user-friendly way. It should show you all of the decisions algorithm made, all of the data algorithm
> used to show you what it has decided to show you the, these posts. And I think the posts don't have
> to be advertising only, which is also important. But I think it should also show you, like, why are
> you seeing this photo from your very old friend? Why are you seeing this photo from a brand? Why are
> you seeing this photo from an influencer? Why are you seeing this, uh, advertising? So I think I
> need to have, like, a multiple examples, and I want this place, like, you know, it shows, why am I
> seeing this? And you click on it, it kind of expands and basically shows you all of the decisions it
> has made, and maybe it even gives you a little, uh, quick access to a different place where you can
> adjust your algorithm somehow. I think adjusting your algorithm part can be a second different
> prototype, so we don't need to design this right now. Uh, but let's just make this feed and see how
> we like that.
